# AI Capability Platform Blueprint

## North star

This product is not a traditional course library. It is a living AI capability system that determines what a learner can actually do, builds the shortest useful learning path, asks the learner to perform realistic work, verifies demonstrated capability, and updates the learner's profile as technology changes.

The current Solid Edge AI name is treated as a legacy/working identity while a permanent brand is researched and cleared. Existing software, course content, checkout, delivery, accounts, and infrastructure remain reusable assets.

## Core product loop

**Assess -> Personalize -> Learn -> Perform -> Verify -> Update -> Advance**

1. **Assess** — measure current capability across practical AI skill areas.
2. **Personalize** — identify the learner's strongest skills, weakest skills, role, goals, and risk level.
3. **Learn** — assign only the lessons needed for the next capability milestone.
4. **Perform** — require realistic work products, not passive completion alone.
5. **Verify** — evaluate the work against transparent criteria and human-approval requirements.
6. **Update** — revise the learner's capability profile based on demonstrated evidence.
7. **Advance** — unlock the next skill, workflow, specialization, or professional track.

## Capability framework

The first version measures six practical dimensions:

1. **AI Foundations** — understands what models can and cannot reliably do.
2. **Instruction Design** — can provide context, constraints, examples, and output requirements.
3. **Verification** — can identify claims, calculations, sources, assumptions, and decisions that require checking.
4. **Workflow Design** — can turn repeatable work into a controlled AI-assisted process.
5. **Automation & Agents** — understands tools, permissions, memory, state, monitoring, approvals, and failure recovery.
6. **Safety & Privacy** — knows when information should not be shared, when human approval is mandatory, and how to reduce operational risk.

Each dimension is scored independently. A learner should never receive one vague 'AI level' that hides important weaknesses.

## Evidence-based progression

Completion is not the same as capability.

Every meaningful milestone should eventually contain:

- a short explanation;
- a realistic task;
- a submitted work product or structured response;
- a rubric showing what good work requires;
- verification and privacy checks;
- a pass, revise, or human-review result;
- a curriculum version and assessment version.

The platform should be able to say what the learner demonstrated, not merely what content they opened.

## Adaptive learning path

The learner's path is generated from:

- capability scores;
- occupation or business role;
- stated goals;
- demonstrated work;
- risk-sensitive weaknesses;
- prerequisite skills;
- curriculum changes.

A learner who already demonstrates a capability should not be forced through unnecessary beginner material. A learner with a verification or privacy weakness should not advance into high-autonomy agent material until that weakness is addressed.

## Learning ladder

### Foundation
Mental models, effective instructions, verification, privacy, and responsible everyday use.

### Builder
Reusable prompts, research, communication, sales, content, process mapping, and measurable workflows.

### Advanced
Automation, tool access, agents, memory, state, approvals, monitoring, security, and recovery.

### Professional
Role- and industry-specific capability tracks. The existing AI Automation for Contractors material becomes the first professional specialization and is reused where it meets the new evidence-based standard.

## Capability profile

Each learner should eventually have a durable profile containing:

- capability score by dimension;
- demonstrated skills;
- evidence/assessment IDs;
- curriculum version;
- last verified date;
- current learning path;
- specialization progress;
- skills that need re-verification after material technology changes.

The long-term credential is a verifiable capability record, not a decorative certificate alone.

## Curriculum freshness

AI changes too quickly for static courses to be treated as permanent truth.

Every lesson and assessment should have:

- version;
- last-reviewed date;
- review owner;
- technology/provider dependencies if applicable;
- change notes;
- re-verification rule when a major change invalidates an old skill demonstration.

The system should eventually flag affected learners when a capability needs refreshing.

## Course standard

Every released learning unit must have:

- a named audience and prerequisite capability;
- measurable learning outcomes;
- short mobile-first lessons;
- a practical exercise using a realistic task;
- verification, privacy, and human-approval guidance where applicable;
- an assessment or evidence requirement;
- a documented passing standard;
- version and last-reviewed date;
- a revision owner and review schedule.

## Business model

### Free
- initial capability assessment;
- capability profile preview;
- selected Foundation lessons;
- starter tasks/templates;
- newsletter and curriculum-change notices.

### Paid individual
- complete adaptive learning paths;
- Builder and Advanced programs;
- professional specializations;
- verified capability assessments;
- durable progress and evidence history.

### Membership later
- continuously updated library;
- new assessments when technology changes;
- labs and advanced releases;
- periodic capability re-verification.

### Business/team plans later
- seat management;
- role-specific capability standards;
- shared progress dashboards;
- verified employee capability records;
- organization-specific learning paths.

Revenue comes from deeper outcomes, verified capability, continuously updated curriculum, and professional tracks — not from selling student data.

## Build sequence

1. Replace the old opportunity calculator with a real capability assessment.
2. Generate a personalized capability profile and recommended path.
3. Redesign the Academy around the Assess -> Learn -> Perform -> Verify loop.
4. Convert Foundation lessons into task-based capability units.
5. Add durable learner accounts and progress storage.
6. Add evidence submissions, rubrics, and pass/revise results.
7. Add capability history and curriculum versioning.
8. Convert the contractor program into the first evidence-based Professional track.
9. Add public capability verification only after identity, assessment integrity, and privacy controls are ready.
10. Research and clear the permanent company/product brand before deeper brand investment.

## Institutional direction

### Stage 1 — Legitimate training business
Maintain legal ownership, support contact, accounting/tax handling, policies, accessibility expectations, refund rules, and curriculum update standards.

### Stage 2 — Verifiable capability platform
Add durable accounts, assessments, evidence records, versioned capability history, correction workflows, privacy requests, and public verification where appropriate.

### Stage 3 — External recognition
Build employer and industry partnerships around demonstrated skills and portfolio evidence. Evaluate continuing-education approval or accreditation only with qualified education counsel and the appropriate bodies. Never imply accreditation, degrees, licenses, or official recognition that has not actually been granted.

## Product principle

The platform should answer one question better than a normal course website:

**What can this person actually do with AI, what evidence proves it, and what should they learn next?**
