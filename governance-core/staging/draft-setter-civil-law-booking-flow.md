# Civil Law Firm — Consultation Booking Flow

Written by: The Setter
Status: PENDING
Date: 2026-11-20

## In plain English

This is the step-by-step flow for booking a consultation with the firm — offering times, confirming the time, and sending reminders. It books meetings only. It never answers a legal question and never promises anything about a case.

---

## How to use this flow

- Follow the steps in order. Every step is a scheduling step — nothing here is legal work.
- If the person asks any legal question at any point, route it to the attorney: "The attorney will answer that question." Do not answer it, and do not delay the booking to discuss it.
- If the person describes a criminal matter, hand it straight to the lawyer and stop. See "If a criminal matter appears" below.
- All message templates in this document are blanks with [bracketed fields] the firm fills in. Nothing is sent without the firm's approval. This document is a draft-only dry run.

## Step 1 — Offer times

When a consultation is to be booked:

1. Check the attorney's calendar for open consultation slots.
2. Offer the caller **two or three** specific options — a day, a date, and a time for each.

> "The attorney has these times open for a consultation: [option 1 — day, date, time], [option 2 — day, date, time], and [option 3 — day, date, time]. Which of those works for you?"

3. If none of the offered times work, ask:

> "What days and times generally work best for you? I will check the attorney's calendar and get back to you."

4. Do not promise a time that is not on the calendar. Do not hold a time without confirming it (see Step 2).

## Step 2 — Confirm the time

Once the caller picks a time:

1. Repeat it back in full: day, date, time, and time zone.
2. Confirm how the consultation will happen: in person at the office, by phone, or by video — whichever the firm offers and the caller prefers.
3. Confirm the contact details to use for the meeting (phone number for a call, email for a video link, office address for an in-person visit).
4. Say plainly:

> "You are booked for a consultation on [day, date] at [time] [time zone], [in person at the office / by phone / by video]."

5. Ask the pre-meeting questions in Step 3.
6. Send the confirmation message (template below) after the call, once the firm has approved it.

**Do not confirm anything about the substance of the matter.** Do not say what the attorney will advise, whether the firm will take the matter, or what the outcome might be. The Setter books the meeting; the attorney handles the law.

## Step 3 — What to ask before the meeting

Ask these before the consultation, so the attorney has what they need. Ask only these scheduling and preparation questions — do not ask legal questions, and do not answer any.

1. > "Is there anything you would like the attorney to know before the meeting?" (Record the answer in the person's own words. Do not interpret it.)
2. > "Do you have any documents related to this matter that you could bring or send ahead of the meeting?" (Record only what kind of documents they name — for example, a contract, a letter, medical records, photos. Do not ask what the documents mean, and do not say which documents matter. That is a question for the lawyer.)
3. > "Is there a deadline or a court date coming up that the attorney should know about before the meeting?" (Record any date in the person's own words. Do not comment on it.)
4. > "Will anyone be coming with you to the consultation?" (Record the name and relationship, if given.)
5. > "What is the best number to reach you at if anything about the meeting changes?"

Record all answers and pass them to the attorney with the booking.

## Step 4 — What to send after booking

After the consultation is booked, send the confirmation message (template below) using the contact method the person prefers. The message goes out only after the firm approves it.

If the person was asked to bring or send documents ahead of the meeting, the confirmation message may list the document types they named — nothing more. Never add a document type the person did not name, and never say what any document means or why it matters.

## Step 5 — Reminders

- Send the **day-before reminder** (template below) the day before the consultation.
- If the person replies that they cannot make it, use the **reschedule wording** (template below) and return to Step 1.
- If the person does not show and does not reply, tell the firm. The Setter does not decide what happens next.

## Templates

Every template below is a blank with [bracketed fields]. The firm fills in the fields and approves the message before anything is sent.

### Confirmation message

> Subject: Your consultation is confirmed — [date]
>
> Dear [first name],
>
> Your consultation with the firm is confirmed for [day, date] at [time] [time zone].
>
> [In person at: office address / By phone — the firm will call you at [phone number] / By video — the meeting link is [video link].]
>
> [If documents were named: You mentioned you may bring [document type(s) named by the person].]
>
> If anything changes and you need to move the meeting, please let us know at [firm phone number] or [firm email address].
>
> Thank you,
> [firm name as the firm writes it]
> [firm phone number]
> [firm email address]

### Day-before reminder

> Subject: Reminder — your consultation tomorrow, [date]
>
> Dear [first name],
>
> This is a reminder that your consultation with the firm is tomorrow, [day, date] at [time] [time zone].
>
> [In person at: office address / By phone — the firm will call you at [phone number] / By video — the meeting link is [video link].]
>
> If you need to move the meeting, please let us know at [firm phone number] or [firm email address].
>
> Thank you,
> [firm name as the firm writes it]

### Reschedule wording

Use when the person asks to move the consultation, or when the firm needs to move it.

> Subject: Rescheduling your consultation
>
> Dear [first name],
>
> We need to move your consultation from [original day, date] at [original time] to a new time.
>
> The following times are available: [option 1 — day, date, time], [option 2 — day, date, time], [option 3 — day, date, time].
>
> Please let us know which of these works for you, or suggest a time that does. Once we confirm a new time, we will send you a new confirmation.
>
> Thank you,
> [firm name as the firm writes it]
> [firm phone number]
> [firm email address]

If the person replies with a time that works, confirm it using Step 2 and send a fresh confirmation message.

## If a criminal matter appears

This team works civil matters only. If, at any point in the booking flow, the person describes a criminal matter — an arrest, criminal charges, a police investigation, a criminal court date — stop the booking flow, hand the person's name and contact information straight to the lawyer, and say:

> "That is a question for the lawyer. I will pass your name and number to the attorney right away."

Do not book, do not advise, do not continue.

## Confidentiality

Everything a person shares while booking stays inside the firm's environment. The Setter does not share booking information with anyone outside the firm and does not discuss one person's matter with another person.

---

## Hard lines

- **No legal advice.** This flow never predicts an outcome, never says anyone has a case, never says anyone should sue, never advises what anyone should do legally, and never explains anyone's legal rights. Every legal question goes to the attorney — "The attorney will answer that question."
- **No licensed act.** Nothing here is filed, signed, or represented as legal work. The Setter books meetings; only the attorney does legal work.
- **Draft-only.** This is a dry-run flow and these templates are drafts. Nothing goes to a client, a court, or an opposing party without the attorney's yes, and no message is sent without the firm's approval.
- **Civil only.** If a criminal matter appears, the flow hands it straight to the lawyer and stops.
- **No firm named, no price named.** This document names no real firm and no price anywhere.
- **Confidentiality.** Client information stays inside the firm's environment.
