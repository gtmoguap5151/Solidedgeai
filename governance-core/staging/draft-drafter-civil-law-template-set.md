# Civil Law Firm — Template Set

Written by: The Drafter
Status: PENDING
Date: 2026-11-20

## In plain English

These are blank shells for the letters and forms a small civil firm sends most — an engagement letter, a records request, a status letter, a demand letter, and an intake confirmation. Each one is a blank the attorney fills in and approves. The Drafter never sends anything itself.

---

## How to use this template set

- Every template below is a **blank shell** with [bracketed fields]. The attorney fills in every field, reviews every word, and approves the finished letter before it goes anywhere.
- **The Drafter never sends anything itself.** Drafting is all the Drafter does. Sending is the attorney's decision, every time.
- No template here contains legal advice, predictions, or statements about anyone's rights. Where a letter would need legal wording, the template leaves a [bracketed field] for the attorney to write.
- If a criminal matter appears in any matter these templates touch, the templates stop — the matter goes straight to the lawyer.
- Confidentiality: drafts stay inside the firm's environment. No draft is shared outside the firm.

## Template 3 — Status letter shell

**The attorney reviews, fills, and approves every word before this letter goes anywhere.**

> [Firm letterhead]
>
> [Date]
>
> [Client full name]
> [Client mailing address]
>
> Re: Status update — [matter description as the attorney writes it]
>
> Dear [client first name],
>
> We are writing with an update on your matter.
>
> **What has happened since our last update:** [Factual update as the attorney writes it — what has been done, what has been received, what has been filed or sent, if anything.]
>
> **What happens next:** [Next steps as the attorney writes them.]
>
> **What we need from you:** [Any documents or information the firm needs from the client, as the attorney writes it — or "Nothing at this time."]
>
> If you have any questions about your matter, the attorney will answer them. Please contact the firm at [firm phone number] or [firm email address].
>
> Thank you,
>
> [Attorney name]
> [Attorney title]
> [Firm name as the firm writes it]

**Rules for this template:**
- The status letter reports facts only — what has happened and what happens next, as the attorney writes it.
- It never predicts an outcome, never says the matter is going well or badly, and never advises the client what to do. Any question of what a development means is a question for the lawyer, answered by the attorney — not by this letter.

## Template 4 — Simple demand-letter shell

**The attorney reviews, fills, and approves every word before this letter goes anywhere.**

> [Firm letterhead]
>
> [Date]
>
> [Recipient full name]
> [Recipient mailing address]
>
> Re: [Matter description as the attorney writes it]
>
> Dear [recipient name],
>
> The firm represents [client full name] in connection with [matter description as the attorney writes it].
>
> **What happened.** [Factual summary as the attorney writes it.]
>
> **What the firm is asking for.** [The demand as the attorney writes it — what the firm asks the recipient to do, and by when.]
>
> **Response requested by:** [Date as the attorney writes it.]
>
> Please direct any response to the firm at [firm mailing address], [firm phone number], or [firm email address]. Please do not contact [client full name] directly about this matter.
>
> Thank you,
>
> [Attorney name]
> [Attorney title]
> [Firm name as the firm writes it]

**Rules for this template:**
- The demand letter contains only facts and the attorney's own demand, as the attorney writes them.
- It never threatens anything the attorney has not written, never states a legal conclusion, and never predicts what will happen if the recipient does not respond. Every word of legal substance is the attorney's.

## Template 5 — Intake confirmation letter

**The attorney reviews, fills, and approves every word before this letter goes anywhere.**

> [Firm letterhead]
>
> [Date]
>
> [Client full name]
> [Client mailing address]
>
> Re: Confirmation — your contact with the firm on [date of intake]
>
> Dear [client first name],
>
> Thank you for contacting the firm on [date of intake]. This letter confirms that we have your information on file.
>
> **What you told us.** You told us your matter concerns [brief description in the client's own words, as recorded at intake].
>
> **What happens next.** [Next step as the firm recorded it — for example, "The attorney will review your information" or "You are scheduled for a consultation on (date, time)."]
>
> **What we need from you.** [Any documents or information the firm asked for at intake — or "Nothing at this time."]
>
> If you have any questions about your matter, the attorney will answer them. Please contact the firm at [firm phone number] or [firm email address].
>
> Thank you,
>
> [Firm name as the firm writes it]

**Rules for this template:**
- This letter confirms only that the firm has the client's information. It does not say the firm has taken the matter, will take the matter, or has formed any view of it.
- It never says the client has a case, never predicts an outcome, and never advises the client what to do.

## If a criminal matter appears

This team works civil matters only. If any matter these templates touch turns out to involve a criminal matter — an arrest, criminal charges, a police investigation, a criminal court date — stop using the templates for that matter and hand it straight to the lawyer.

---

## Hard lines

- **No legal advice.** These templates never predict an outcome, never say anyone has a case, never say anyone should sue, never advise what anyone should do legally, and never explain anyone's legal rights. Every legal question goes to the attorney — "The attorney will answer that question."
- **No licensed act.** Nothing here is filed, signed, or represented as legal work. The Drafter drafts blank shells; only the attorney does legal work, and the attorney reviews, fills, and approves every word before anything goes anywhere. The Drafter never sends anything itself.
- **Draft-only.** These are blank draft shells. Nothing goes to a client, a court, or an opposing party without the attorney's yes.
- **Civil only.** If a criminal matter appears, the templates stop and the matter goes straight to the lawyer.
- **No firm named, no price named.** This document names no real firm and no price anywhere.
- **Confidentiality.** Client information stays inside the firm's environment.
