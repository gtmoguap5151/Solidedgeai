## In plain English

Franklin brought five business ideas. I checked the money on each one.
Every idea costs $0 in cash to start — the only cost is Matt's time.
So the real question on all five is the same: how many dollars per hour
of Matt's time, and how soon does the first dollar land. The prices in
the brief are Franklin's guesses. No customer has paid any of them yet.
That is the first fact, and it colors all the rest.

## The one number that matters

Revenue across everything Matt owns is $0. No sale has landed anywhere
yet. Every price below is proposed, not proven. The first person to pay
any of these prices is the test that settles all five ideas at once.

Money cost is $0 on all five (the brief says so, and I take it at its
word). Money margin is therefore the full price on every sale. The
margin that matters is time: dollars per hour of Matt's work.

## 1. Grant writing for small churches and nonprofits

**Price:** $150–$300 per application (the brief's range for a first one).
**Cost to run:** $0 in money. Time per application: NOT stated in the
brief. That is the hole in this idea. The hourly pay depends entirely
on how long one application takes, and nobody has timed one yet.
**Margin:** The full fee — $150 to $300 per job, minus nothing but time.
**First-dollar path:** One pastor says yes to one application. Matt
writes it, the church pays on delivery. He is paid for the writing
whether the grant lands or not — that is the best payment term of the
five. But no paying client exists yet. The Walmart Spark Good draft now
underway is a sample, not a sale.
**Verdict: worth Matt's time.** Highest fee of the five, best payment
terms, and he has already done the work — ShopHand, Verizon, the NSF
pitch are real filings, not theory. The risk is trust: some boards will
say no when they learn where he is. That costs time, not money, and
time is the only cost here. **What would settle the open number:** time
the first application, start to finish. If one application takes a
full day, that is $150–$300 a day. If it takes three days, cut the
hourly pay by two-thirds and re-check.

## 2. Resumes and cover letters for tradespeople and returning citizens

**Price:** $49 for a resume, $79 for resume plus cover letter.
**Cost to run:** $0 in money. Time per resume: the brief says "writes
it that day" — same-day turnaround, not hours. It also says if a resume
takes three days, the numbers do not work. So the workable window is
one day or less. At $49, one hour of work pays $49 an hour; three hours
pays about $16 an hour. The brief does not say how long one actually
takes.
**Margin:** The full $49 or $79, minus nothing but time.
**First-dollar path:** The fastest of the five. One man with a welding
ticket and no resume pays $49, Matt writes it that day. No gate, no
waiting, no third party to convince.
**Verdict: worth trying, thin.** Fastest first dollar, and the edge is
real — he knows the trades and he knows reentry, and a template does
not. But the market is crowded: Fiverr and AI tools write resumes for
less. The brief is honest about it — finding people is the real work,
and finding people is unpaid. Price pressure is real: below $49, the
brief says it is not worth the hour. **What would settle the open
number:** time three resumes. If they take an hour each, this is good
money. If they take three hours each, it is not.

## 3. Family-history research reports

**Price:** $49 for a short report (one line, three generations), $99
for the full report with documents.
**Cost to run:** $0 in money — free records only (FamilySearch, National
Archives, state archives). Time is the whole cost, and the brief names
it plainly: a $49 report that takes six hours pays $8 an hour.
**Margin:** The full $49 or $99, minus nothing but time.
**First-dollar path:** One customer with a Harlan County name pays $49.
No channel exists yet to find that customer — the brief names no road
to buyers. The first dollar waits on the first customer finding him.
**Verdict: not worth it at $49 unless the research is fast.** The brief's
own math is the verdict: six hours at $8 an hour is a poor trade. This
only works if a short report can be held to about two hours — then it
pays about $25 an hour. His Harlan County knowledge helps speed, but
speed is unproven. The $99 full report is the better unit if the
research time does not double with it — also unproven. **What would
settle the open number:** time one short report, honestly, start to
finish. If it is over three hours, the price has to rise or the idea
has to go.

## 4. The Inside Guide — for families of incarcerated people

**Price:** $19 for the guide (PDF), same as the Deer Season Playbook.
**Cost to run:** $0 in money. Time: the guide is written once, from
what he knows. After that, each sale costs nothing but the delivery,
which already exists. This is the only one of the five where the work
is done once and the unit cost is $0 per sale.
**Margin:** The full $19 per sale, minus nothing but time already spent
writing. In theory the hourly pay is unlimited. In practice it is $0
until someone buys.
**First-dollar path:** The longest of the five — the brief says so
itself. One mother, one wife, one brother buys the guide. But these
families are not browsing a hunting store, and the guide needs its own
road to them. That road does not exist yet. No first-dollar date can
be named.
**Verdict: worth writing, not worth counting on.** The economics are
the best of the five on paper — write once, sell forever — and it is
the one only Matt can write. But uniqueness does not pay until
distribution exists, and distribution is the whole job here. This is a
shelf product, not a first dollar. Write it when there is a spare day.
Do not wait on it for money.

## 5. Custom memory books — family stories, written to order

**Price:** $49 for a short book (up to 10 pages), $99 for the full book
with photos placed.
**Cost to run:** $0 in money. Time is the whole cost, and the brief
names it: a $49 book that takes five hours is thin pay — that is under
$10 an hour. Rewrites eat the profit, and taste is personal — one
family will want it rewritten.
**Margin:** The full $49 or $99, minus nothing but time.
**First-dollar path:** One daughter orders a book for her father's 70th
birthday, fills in the questionnaire, Matt writes the book, she pays on
delivery. The market is seasonal — Christmas and birthdays, quiet the
rest of the year. Christmas is a month away, so the window is open
now and closes fast.
**Verdict: worth one test order, not a line of business.** Hand work
does not scale, the hourly pay is thin under the brief's own time
example, and the market goes quiet after Christmas. Take one order,
time it, and let the number decide. The questionnaire rule in the brief
is the right one: no complete questionnaire, no order.

## Rankings

**By first-dollar speed** (fastest first):
1. Resumes — same day, one $49 payment, no gate.
2. Grant writing — first dollar on delivery of the first application,
   but the first yes from a pastor is the gate, and no paying client
   exists yet.
3. Memory books — first dollar on delivery, but the customer has to
   find him and finish the questionnaire first; Christmas window is
   open now.
4. Family-history reports — first dollar on delivery, but no channel
   to find the first customer.
5. The Inside Guide — longest road; no road to buyers exists yet.

**By hourly pay** (best first, on the brief's own numbers):
1. Grant writing — $150–$300 per job; the hourly pay depends on time
   per application, which is unmeasured. Best per-unit fee by far.
2. Resumes — $49 per unit, fast turnaround; $16–$49 an hour depending
   on time per resume, which is unmeasured.
3. The Inside Guide — $0 per sale after the writing is done; the
   hourly pay is whatever the sales turn out to be, and sales are
   unproven. Best in theory, $0 in practice until distribution exists.
4. Family-history reports — $8 an hour under the brief's own six-hour
   example; only works if a short report holds to about two hours.
5. Memory books — under $10 an hour under the brief's own five-hour
   example, before rewrites.

## What is thin or unproven in the brief

- **Every price is a guess.** No customer has paid any of these five
  prices. The first paying customer settles all of them.
- **No time is measured anywhere.** The brief gives time examples for
  two ideas (six hours, five hours) but no measured time for any of
  them. Hourly pay — the only margin that matters — is unproven on
  all five. What settles it: time the first unit of each, honestly.
- **No buyer channel exists for three of the five.** Family-history,
  the Inside Guide, and memory books name no road to the first
  customer. Finding buyers is unpaid work, and the brief does not
  price it.
- **Payment processing is not in the brief.** If sales run through
  Stripe, a fee comes off every sale. The brief names no fee. What
  settles it: check the Stripe account's fee schedule before quoting
  any price as net.
- **The grant-writing fee range is wide** ($150–$300). The brief does
  not say what sets the fee within the range. What settles it: the
  first three paid applications.

## Bottom line

Grant writing is the money. Resumes are the fastest dollar. The Inside
Guide is the best economics on paper and the worst road to a buyer.
Family-history and memory books pay $8–$10 an hour under the brief's
own time examples — they are craft, not income, unless the time comes
down. None of the five is proven until one person pays. The first
paying customer is worth more than all five briefs.
