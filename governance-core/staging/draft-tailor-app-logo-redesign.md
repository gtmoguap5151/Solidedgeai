## Rivet Reach — Direction A: Two Plates, One Rivet

Photo: ~/workspace/brand-logos/rivet-reach/redesign/rr-a-two-plates.png

Two steel plates overlap along a diagonal seam, and one big copper rivet sits where they join. The idea is the business itself: a homeowner on one side, a contractor on the other, and Rivet Reach is the rivet that joins them. Dark version is steel plates on deep green; light version is a green plate and a steel plate on bone.

- **Why it is not generic:** nobody else's logo is two plates and a rivet — the mark IS what the company does. No shield, no badge, no stock shape.
- **At 48px:** two-tone square with a bright copper dot in the middle. Reads instantly.
- **Watch-out:** the most abstract of the three — it does not spell out the name. The app name shows under the icon on the home screen, so the icon does not need letters.

## Rivet Reach — Direction B: The R as Steel

Photo: ~/workspace/brand-logos/rivet-reach/redesign/rr-b-steel-r.png

A bold letter R built from plate steel — thick stem, solid bowl, diagonal leg — with three copper rivets driven down the stem. Dark version is silver steel on deep green; light version is deep green on bone.

- **Why it is not generic:** the letter is the structure. The rivets are not decoration sprinkled on a badge — they are driven into the steel the letter is made of.
- **At 48px:** the R still reads, and the three copper dots still show as a row down the stem.
- **Watch-out:** a single letter is the most common logo idea there is. What saves it is the construction — plate steel and driven rivets, not a typed font.

## Rivet Reach — Direction C: The Domed Head

Photo: ~/workspace/brand-logos/rivet-reach/redesign/rr-c-domed-head.png

One giant copper rivet head, up close, filling the icon — domed shading, a stamped ring near the rim, a small driving mark in the center. The rivet itself is the logo.

- **Why it is not generic:** it is a macro view nobody else uses. At a glance it is unmistakably a rivet, not a badge, not a letter, not a dot.
- **At 48px:** the strongest of the three at small size — one bold copper circle. Nothing to get lost.
- **Watch-out:** the boldest and simplest, which means the least information. It says "rivet" and nothing else — the name carries the rest.

## Rivet Reach — Round 2, Direction A: The Roofline R

Photo: ~/workspace/brand-logos/rivet-reach/redesign/rr2-a-roofline-r.png

The letter R, but its bowl is a peaked roof — the top of the R IS the house. The bowl's counter is an arched doorway with a thin brass frame, open at the bottom of the bowl. The leg is emerald, kicking down from the bowl. Dark version is bone on deep green; light version is deep green on bone.

- **Why it is not generic:** the roof is not sitting on top of a letter — it IS the letter's bowl. Take the roof away and there is no R left. The doorway is the R's own counter, not a house icon pasted beside a letter.
- **At 48px:** the R still reads, the roof peak still shows, the doorway is a small dark arch. The brass frame is the first detail to go small, which is fine — the door shape holds without it.
- **Watch-out:** the most "letter-first" of the three — the house reads second. If Matt wants the house to hit first, B is the pick.

## Rivet Reach — Round 2, Direction B: The House Is an R

Photo: ~/workspace/brand-logos/rivet-reach/redesign/rr2-b-house-r.png

A house front that IS the letter: the left wall is the R's stem, the round gable window is the R's bowl, and a brass diagonal from mid-stem is the R's leg. ## What is true in every mockup

- **No wordmark in the icons.** The phone shows the app name under the icon, so the icon carries the mark alone. The full Blackpine lockup (faceted pine + BLACKPINE + ROOTS RAN DEEP) stays exactly as locked for the store and the app header — the icon adapts the pine for small size, it does not change the mark.
- **The locked mark is untouched.** The faceted pine in these icons is a simplified drawing of the locked mark (same tiers, same wedge, same amber), not a new mark and not the old triangle tree. The triangle tree appears nowhere.
- **No shield, no badge, no stock shape** in any Rivet Reach direction. Round 1 kept the rivet in all three — as the join (A), as the fastener in the steel (B), as the whole mark (C). **Round 2 has NO rivet at all** — Matt ruled it out entirely. Round 2 is R + house, fused: the roof is the bowl (A), the house is the letter (B), the doorway is the R (C). The only round shape in round 2 is B's gable window, drawn as a ring so it reads as a window, never as a rivet head.
- **The two businesses stay separate.** Rivet Reach and Blackpine Field share no design, no color story, no layout — each logo stands on its own.
- **No face, no likeness of Matt anywhere.** No image generation was used for any of this — every mark was drawn from scratch as vector shapes (PIL + numpy), which is also why every edge is exact at every size.
- **Every direction was checked at true size.** All six round-1 directions and all three round-2 directions read at 48px and 64px on dark and light. Each direction sheet shows the true-size icons plus a 4x enlargement of the 48px render so the small-size read can be judged honestly.
