# Civil Law Firm — Dry-Run Intake Script

Written by: The Intake
Status: PENDING
Date: 2026-11-20

## In plain English

This is a practice script for taking a new caller's information — name, contact details, and what their matter is about. It is not legal advice, and it never answers a legal question: every question about the law goes to the attorney.

---

## How to use this script

- Read the questions in order. Do not skip ahead.
- Write down the caller's answers exactly as they say them. Do not reword, summarize, or interpret what they tell you.
- If the caller asks any question about the law, their rights, or what they should do: **stop, and route it to the attorney** using the wording in the "Routing legal questions" section below.
- If the caller describes a criminal matter (arrest, charges, police, a criminal court date): **stop the intake, hand it straight to the lawyer, and do not continue.** See "If a criminal matter appears" below.
- Nothing in this script is sent anywhere. It is a draft-only dry run. No live callers, no live clients.

## Opening

> "Hello, thank you for calling the firm. My name is [intake worker name]. I can take down your information and make sure the attorney sees it. I am not a lawyer and I cannot answer legal questions, but the attorney will. May I start by taking your name?"

## The questions, in order

Ask each question, wait for the answer, and record it before moving on.

### 1. Caller name

> "What is your full name, please?"

- Record: full legal name (first, middle if given, last). Spell it back to the caller to confirm.
- Ask: "Is there another name you go by, or a name you were known by before?" Record any answer.

### 2. Contact information

> "What is the best phone number to reach you at?"

> "Is there a second number, in case we cannot reach you at the first?"

> "What email address would you like us to use?"

> "What is your mailing address?"

- Record: primary phone, secondary phone (if any), email, full mailing address.
- Ask: "How do you prefer we contact you — phone, email, or mail?" Record the answer.

### 3. What the matter is about, in their own words

> "In your own words, can you tell me what this matter is about?"

- Let the caller speak. Do not interrupt, do not ask leading questions, do not suggest words for them.
- Record what they say in their own words. If something is unclear, ask only: "Could you say a little more about that?" — never "Do you mean [X]?"
- Do not ask whether they think they have a case. Do not ask what outcome they want from the law.

### 4. Who else is involved

> "Are there other people or companies involved in this matter? If so, what are their names?"

- Record every name the caller gives: people, businesses, insurers, agencies. Ask for spelling where needed.
- These names go to the firm for the conflict check (see "Conflict-flag checklist" below). The Intake does not decide whether a conflict exists — the firm does.

### 5. How urgent it is

> "How urgent would you say this is? Is there anything happening soon that we should know about?"

- Record the caller's own sense of urgency in their words.
- Then ask the deadline question directly (next item).

### 6. Whether they have a deadline coming up

> "Do you have any deadline coming up — a date by which something has to happen?"

> "Do you already have a court date set? If so, when and where?"

- Record any date, the court or office named, and what the date is for — in the caller's own words.
- **Do not tell the caller whether a deadline matters, whether it can be extended, or what happens if it passes.** That is a question for the lawyer.

### 7. Whether anyone is hurt and needs care

> "Is anyone hurt and in need of medical care right now?"

- If yes: record who is hurt and what care they need, in the caller's words. **Flag it for the firm immediately** (see "Urgency flags" below). Do not give medical advice. If the caller describes an emergency happening right now, tell them to call emergency services.
- Do not ask how the injury happened in a way that seeks fault, and do not comment on who is responsible.

### 8. How they heard of the firm

> "How did you hear about the firm?"

- Record the answer (referral name, online search, advertisement, another client, etc.). If a person referred them, record that person's name.

## Closing

> "Thank you. I have your information down and I will make sure the attorney sees it. The attorney will be the one to answer any legal questions and to decide next steps. Is there anything else you would like me to pass along to the attorney?"

- Record any final message in the caller's own words.
- Do not promise a callback time, an outcome, or that the firm will take the matter. If the caller asks when they will hear back, say: "I will pass your information to the attorney. The attorney or the firm will be in touch."
- Do not promise the firm will take the case. Do not say the matter sounds strong, weak, or anything else.

## Urgency flags

If any of the following appears in the caller's answers, flag the intake for the firm **immediately** and mark it URGENT. Flagging means: tell the firm, in writing, that this flag is present. It never means advising the caller.

| Flag | What to do |
|---|---|
| A deadline within days | Mark URGENT. Write the date and what it is for, in the caller's words, at the top of the intake record. Hand it to the firm right away. Do not tell the caller whether the deadline can be met or extended. |
| A court date already set | Mark URGENT. Record the date, court, and what the date is for. Hand it to the firm right away. Do not tell the caller what to do about the court date. |
| Someone hurt and needing care | Mark URGENT. Record who is hurt and what care they need. Hand it to the firm right away. Do not give medical advice. If it is an emergency happening now, tell the caller to call emergency services. |

The Intake does not decide what the firm does about a flag. The Intake flags it and hands it over.

## Conflict-flag checklist — for the FIRM to check

The Intake collects the names. **The firm checks them.** The Intake never decides whether a conflict exists, never tells the caller a conflict exists, and never tells the caller the firm cannot help them.

The firm checks the intake record against:

- [ ] **Same names as existing clients** — Is the caller's name (or any name they go by) already a client of the firm?
- [ ] **Opposing party names** — Is any person or company the caller named as involved already a client of the firm, or already on the other side of an existing matter?
- [ ] **Related matters** — Is there an existing matter involving the same people, the same property, the same incident, or the same subject?
- [ ] **Referral source** — If the caller was referred by an existing client, does that referral create any connection the firm needs to note?

The firm records the result of each check. If the firm finds a conflict, the firm decides what happens next — not the Intake, and not this script.

## Routing legal questions

The Intake never answers a legal question. If the caller asks one, use this wording:

> "That is a question for the lawyer. The attorney will answer that question. I will write it down so the attorney sees exactly what you asked."

Then record the question word for word and pass it to the attorney.

Legal questions include, but are not limited to: whether the caller has a case, whether they should sue, what their rights are, what the law says about their situation, what a deadline means, what a document means, what they should do next, and what outcome to expect. **All of them go to the attorney.**

## If a criminal matter appears

This team works civil matters only. If the caller describes anything criminal — an arrest, criminal charges, a police investigation, a criminal court date, being accused of a crime — do this and nothing else:

1. Stop the intake.
2. Say: "That is a question for the lawyer. I will pass your name and number to the attorney right away."
3. Record the caller's name and contact information only.
4. Hand it straight to the lawyer.

Do not ask further questions about the criminal matter. Do not advise the caller. Do not continue the script.

## Confidentiality

Everything the caller says stays inside the firm's environment. The Intake does not share intake information with anyone outside the firm, does not discuss one caller's matter with another caller, and does not send intake records anywhere outside the firm.

---

## Hard lines

- **No legal advice.** This script never predicts an outcome, never says anyone has a case, never says anyone should sue, never advises what anyone should do legally, and never explains anyone's legal rights. Every legal question goes to the attorney.
- **No licensed act.** Nothing here is filed, signed, or represented as legal work. The Intake collects information; only the attorney does legal work.
- **Draft-only.** This is a dry-run script. Nothing in it goes to a client, a court, or an opposing party without the attorney's yes.
- **Civil only.** If a criminal matter appears, the script hands it straight to the lawyer and stops.
- **No firm named, no price named.** This document names no real firm and no price anywhere.
- **Confidentiality.** Client information stays inside the firm's environment.
