## What the team costs to run

**Known — $0 spent so far.**
- The brain today is rented Gemini (gemini-3.5-flash) on the free tier,
  through the Worker's /chat endpoint. Cost so far: $0.
- Training so far is $0 — three runs on free Colab (T4), $0 each. The
  run-2 adapter (8/8, the best) cost nothing to build.
- The eight workers are registered and their drafts are written. No
  cash has gone into the team.

**NOT known — and these are the numbers that decide the margin:**
- **What the brain costs at real volume.** The free Gemini tier has a
  daily limit — the Worker already maps 429s to "the AI daily limit
  was reached." A paying firm's daily volume is unknown. If a firm
  burns through the free tier, the paid tier costs money, and the
  amount is not in any record I have. What settles it: run the team
  through a simulated firm's week and count the calls, then price the
  paid tier against that count.
- **What hosting the trained adapter costs.** The Keystone's Workers AI
  hosting plan is owed and has no file yet. The model-path map said
  ~650 replies a day free on Workers AI — that figure is from the map,
  not from a live test. What settles it: the Keystone's plan, and a
  live usage test.
- **What support costs in Matt's time.** Onboarding a firm, setting up
  their templates, answering the attorney's questions — that is Matt's
  time, and it is the real cost of this package. No number exists for
  it. What settles it: the first firm's first month, timed honestly.
- **What Lincoln's avatar costs.** Matt's bar is a full moving avatar,
  like Xena's — not a portrait, not clips. That build is not ordered
  and not priced. If the avatar is part of the package, its cost is
  unknown. What settles it: a build quote, when Matt orders the build.
- **Payment processing.** If firms pay through Stripe, a fee comes off
  every payment. The fee schedule is not in this draft's sources. What
  settles it: check the Stripe account's fee schedule.

The honest summary: the team costs $0 today because nobody is using
it. The cost at real use is unknown. Any price set now is set blind

## Price recommendation — for Matt's eyes alone

**I recommend $99/month for the first firm, month-to-month, as a
founding price — and I recommend it be re-set after three months of
proven use.**

Why $99:
- It is Matt's own anchor, and it sits at the bottom of the
  unverified reference range. Starting low is honest for a product no
  firm has ever run.
- The first firm is a proof case, not a profit case. Its job is to
  prove the team works in a real office and to produce the first
  measured usage numbers — which is what the cost side is missing.
- At $99 a month, the first firm pays $1,188 in a year. That is real
  money against $0 revenue today, and it buys the proof the next
  price is set on.

Why not higher now:
- The $100–$600 range is unverified. Pricing at the top of an
  unverified range is guessing with someone else's money.
- The team is unproven. No firm has run it. A firm paying $300 or
  $600 a month for an unproven team is a firm that will leave the
  first month something breaks.
- The run cost is unknown. Until usage is measured, a high price
  could still be a thin margin if the brain costs more than expected
  at volume.

Why not lower than $99:
- Below $99, the support time alone — onboarding, templates, the
  attorney's questions — eats the fee. $99 is the floor that keeps
  the work worth Matt's hours.
- A price below the Rivet Reach anchor also tells the firm the team
  is worth less than the contractor product. It is not.

After three months of proven use, the price is re-set on real numbers:
measured usage cost, measured support time, and what the first firm
says the team is worth to them. If the team is doing real office work
every day, the case for moving toward the middle of the reference
range is made by the firm's own experience — not by me guessing.

**This recommendation is for Matt alone. No price goes to any firm
until Matt approves it. No firm is contacted until Matt says go.**
