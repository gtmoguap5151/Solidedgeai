# The Ledger Hand — Civil Law Billing Drafts

Written by: The Ledger Hand
Status: PENDING
Date: 2026-11-20

## In plain English

This document holds the blank forms the firm uses to draft time entries and invoices. It is a set of empty shells with labels on every line — the firm fills in the numbers and approves every one before anything goes to a client. It is not a bill, it is not a price list, and it never sends anything anywhere on its own.

---

## Time-entry draft format

One entry per task. The attorney or the person who did the work fills in the fields; the Ledger Hand keeps the entries organized by matter and by date.

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Date | [date work was done] |
| Matter number | [matter number] |
| Client last name | [client last name] |
| Person doing the work | [name and role] |
| Task description | [what was done, in plain words — e.g. "drafted a letter to the other side," "reviewed the intake documents," "phone call with client about next steps"] |
| Time spent | [hours and minutes] |
| Notes | [anything else the attorney wants recorded about this task] |

Rules for time entries:

- Write the task description so the client can understand it. No shorthand the client would not follow.
- Record time honestly, in the firm's own rounding rule as the attorney sets it. The Ledger Hand never rounds, adjusts, or estimates time.
- No rates and no dollar amounts are filled in at this stage. Those are added only by the attorney or by the firm's billing person, and only when the invoice is being prepared.
- If a task touches a legal question, the entry describes the work done, not the legal answer. The entry never states legal advice.

## Invoice draft shell

Every invoice starts from this shell. All fields are blank until the firm fills them in and the attorney approves the draft.

> **[Firm name — to be filled in by the firm]**
> **[Firm address — to be filled in by the firm]**
> **[Firm phone and email — to be filled in by the firm]**
>
> **INVOICE — DRAFT**
>
> Invoice number: [invoice number]
> Invoice date: [date]
> Due date: [due date, as set by the firm]
>
> Bill to:
> [client full name]
> [client address]
>
> Matter: [matter number] — [short matter description]
>
> | Date | Description of work | Time | Amount |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | [date] | [task description from the time entry] | [time spent] | [amount — filled in by the firm] |
> | [date] | [task description from the time entry] | [time spent] | [amount — filled in by the firm] |
> | [date] | [task description from the time entry] | [time spent] | [amount — filled in by the firm] |
>
> Expenses:
> | Date | Description | Amount |
> |---|---|---|
> | [date] | [expense description, e.g. filing fee, copying] | [amount — filled in by the firm] |
>
> **Total due: [total — filled in by the firm]**
>
> Payment instructions: [how the client pays, as set by the firm]
> Questions about this invoice: [firm contact, as set by the firm]

Rules for invoices:

- The Ledger Hand drafts the shell and fills in the time entries. The firm fills in every number.
- The attorney approves the full draft before the invoice goes to any client.
- If a client asks about a charge, the Ledger Hand does not answer on its own. The question goes to the attorney or to the firm's billing person.
- No invoice is ever sent by the Ledger Hand. The firm sends it, after approval.

## Payment-reminder wording

Two drafts, for the firm to use after the attorney approves. The firm fills in the bracketed fields.

### First reminder — friendly

> Subject: A friendly reminder about invoice [invoice number]
>
> Dear [client first name],
>
> I hope you are doing well. This is a friendly reminder that invoice [invoice number], dated [invoice date], for matter [matter number], is now due. The amount shown on the invoice is [total due].
>
> If you have already sent payment, please disregard this note and thank you.
>
> If you have any questions about the invoice, please contact [firm contact] at [firm phone or email].
>
> Thank you,
> [firm name]
> [firm contact]

### Second reminder — firmer

> Subject: Second reminder — invoice [invoice number]
>
> Dear [client first name],
>
> We wrote to you on [date of first reminder] about invoice [invoice number], dated [invoice date], for matter [matter number]. Our records show the amount of [total due] is still outstanding.
>
> Please send payment by [date, as set by the firm]. If payment has already been sent, please let us know so we can update our records.
>
> If you would like to discuss the invoice, please contact [firm contact] at [firm phone or email].
>
> Thank you,
> [firm name]
> [firm contact]

Rules for payment reminders:

- The Ledger Hand drafts the reminder. The attorney approves it before it is sent.
- The first reminder goes out only when the firm says the invoice is overdue. The second reminder goes out only when the firm says the first one went unanswered.
- Reminders state the amount and the date only. They never threaten, never predict legal consequences, and never suggest what the firm or the client should do legally. Any question about consequences is a question for the lawyer.
- If a client disputes a charge, the Ledger Hand records the dispute and passes it to the attorney. It does not argue the charge.

## What the Ledger Hand never does

- Never sets a rate, a fee, or a price. Never names a dollar amount on its own.
- Never sends an invoice or a reminder. The firm sends, after the attorney approves.
- Never gives legal advice about billing, fees, or payment disputes. That is a question for the lawyer.
- Never discusses one client's billing with another client.

## Hard lines

- Draft-only: every number is filled in and approved by the firm before anything goes to a client. No price, rate, or dollar amount is named in this document.
- Nothing in this document is legal advice. Any legal question goes to the attorney: "That is a question for the lawyer."
- Nothing goes to a client, a court, or an opposing party without the attorney's yes.
- This is a civil-law team only. If a criminal matter appears, the Ledger Hand hands it straight to the lawyer and stops.
- No real firm is named here. Client information stays inside the firm's environment.
