## The program — verified, not guessed

**Program:** Walmart Spark Good Local Grants (Walmart Foundation)
**Source:** walmart.org — Local Grants guidelines page, read November 20, 2026
**Award:** $250 to $5,000, one grant per facility per fiscal year
**Open now:** Yes. FY27 Cycle 3 runs August 1 – December 31, 2026.
**Deadline — read this twice:** The same guidelines page says Cycle 3 runs to
December 31 AND that "the last day to submit a grant proposal will be
November 30." Two dates on one page. The safe deadline is **November 30,
2026** — ten days from today. A real client must apply by November 30.
**Who can apply:** A church or other faith-based organization "with a proposed
project that benefits the community at large, such as food pantries, soup
kitchens and clothing closets" — and verified by Deed. A church food pantry
is named in the funder's own words.
**What the church must have:** A Spark Good account on Walmart.com, verified
by Walmart's partner Deed. Only an authorized user of the church's account
may apply. The church must serve the same area as the Walmart it applies to.
**What the funder looks for:** Mission-aligned (Walmart says plainly it often
funds hunger relief, being a grocer), builds trust, and effectiveness — it
must be clear what the church does, how the money will be used, and what
impact it will have. The store manager reviews and recommends.
**If approved:** Funds come by electronic payment; the church must supply
banking details within 21 days of being told it was recommended, or the grant
is rejected. The funder may adjust the amount without notice.
**The funder's own warning:** "Submission of an application does not
guarantee funding." That sentence belongs in every conversation with a client.

## Why this program first

It is the right first job for three honest reasons. One, a church food pantry
is exactly what the funder names — the fit is plain, not stretched. Two, the
application is short — a few written questions on the Spark Good form — so a
first draft can be complete and clean. Three, the deadline is ten days out,
which is the honest truth about this business: grant work is deadline work,
and the first client must move fast. The risk is just as plain: the church
must finish Deed verification before it can apply, and that is the church's
hands, not Matt's.

## THE SAMPLE APPLICATION

*Everything below the line is the sample. The church is fictional. The
program facts above are real.*

---

**Applicant (fictional):** Pine Mountain Community Church, Harlan County,
Kentucky — a 501(c)(3) congregation of about 90 members.
**Project:** The Clover Fork Food Pantry — winter stock for a church pantry
serving its neighbors.
**Amount requested:** $5,000 (the top of the program's range)
**Facility applied to:** The Walmart Supercenter serving Harlan County
**Contact (fictional):** Pastor Ruth Caudill, authorized user of the church's
Spark Good account

### Project summary

Pine Mountain Community Church runs the Clover Fork Food Pantry out of its
fellowship hall, open every Saturday morning. The pantry serves about 60
Harlan County families a month — working families, older folks on fixed
incomes, and miners' widows. We ask Walmart for $5,000 to stock the pantry
through the winter: shelf-stable food, a chest freezer so we can take frozen
meat donations, and shelving to hold it all. Every dollar goes to food and
the means to keep it. No salaries, no building work, no overhead — the pantry
is run by volunteers from the congregation.

### Statement of need

Harlan County is coal country, and the coal work that fed this county for
generations is mostly gone. The families who come to our pantry are not
strangers — they are our neighbors, our kin, the people we sit beside in the
pews. Winter is the hard season: heating bills rise, work thins out, and the
pantry's shelves empty faster than we can fill them. Last winter we turned
families away twice in January because the shelves were bare. A pantry that
runs dry in January is a promise broken. This grant keeps the promise — food
on the shelves from December through March, the four hardest months.

### Project plan

- **December:** Buy shelf-stable staples in bulk (rice, beans, canned goods,
  peanut butter, pasta) and install the chest freezer and shelving.
- **December – March:** Pantry open every Saturday, 9 a.m. to noon. Each
  family takes one box a week — enough for roughly five days of meals.
- **March:** Count it up — families served, boxes given, pounds of food moved
  — and report back to the congregation and to Walmart.
- **Volunteers:** Twelve congregation members rotate the Saturday shifts. No
  one is paid. The pastor oversees the pantry and keeps the books.

### Budget — $5,000 requested

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Shelf-stable food, bought in bulk for the winter | $3,000 |
| One chest freezer (to accept frozen meat donations) | $800 |
| Fresh and frozen food through the winter | $700 |
| Shelving and pantry supplies (boxes, bags, labels) | $500 |
| **Total** | **$5,000** |

### Expected outcomes

- 60 families a month served, December through March — about 240 family
  visits across the winter.
- About 960 food boxes given out (one box per family per week).
- Zero Saturdays closed for empty shelves — the promise we broke twice last
  winter, kept this one.
- Every box logged in the pantry book, so the count is real and checkable.

### Why this fits Walmart's own criteria

- **Mission-aligned:** Walmart says it often funds hunger relief because it
  is a grocer. This is hunger relief, in the store's own service area.
- **Builds trust:** The pantry has run every Saturday for three years on
  volunteers. The church is known in the county; the pastor's name is on the
  account.
- **Effectiveness:** The money buys food and the means to keep it. The
  outcomes are counted in boxes and families, not in promises.

### What the church must do to submit

1. Hold a Spark Good account on Walmart.com, verified by Deed, with the
   pastor (or another authorized user) able to sign in.
2. Submit through the Spark Good form to the Walmart serving Harlan County,
   by **November 30, 2026**.
3. If recommended, supply banking details within 21 days for electronic
   payment, and agree to Walmart's logo and recognition guidelines.

## The fee math — for Adamo's money check

The fee is for the writing, win or lose. The church pays when the draft is
delivered. No approval is promised, and none can be — Walmart decides.

**Hours on this job (honest count):**
- Research — finding the program, reading the guidelines, checking the
  church's eligibility: ~1 hour
- Writing — the full application draft above: ~2 hours
- Submission guidance and one round of revisions: ~1 hour
- **Total: ~4 hours**

**Effective hourly rate at 4 hours:**

| Fee | Per hour |
|---|---|
| $150 (first-job price) | $37.50 |
| $200 | $50.00 |
| $300 (standard price) | $75.00 |

If revisions run long and the job takes 5 hours, $150 pays $30 an hour —
thin. The fee should cover one round of revisions; a second round is a
second fee, agreed in advance.

**Franklin's honest word on the price:** This is the lightest kind of grant
work — a short form, a few written questions. The fee is mostly the research
and the writing, and the church paying not to do it themselves. $150 is the
right price for the first one: it proves the service and earns a testimonial.
$300 is the right price once there is a win to show. Do not charge $300 for
the first job — a printer does not charge full price for his first page.

**What the client gets for the fee:** A complete, ready-to-submit
application, the program facts checked, and the submission steps spelled out.
What the client does NOT get: a promise of approval, Deed verification done
for them, or the submission itself — the church's authorized user submits it,
because only they can sign into the church's account.
